How it works
01

You paste an address

Nothing is connected and nothing is signed. The address is all Clearing ever receives from you.

02

The chain is read

The mint account, the total supply and the twenty largest token accounts are fetched straight from Solana nodes.

03

You see who holds the keys

Live authorities, concentration outside the pool, and accounts that trace back to the same owner.

What the tool sees. Mint and freeze authorities, total supply, and the twenty largest token accounts with their real owner. When several accounts in the top 20 share one owner, it gets flagged — that is the most common bundling pattern. Liquidity pools are detected and excluded from concentration, so a pool holding most of the supply does not read as a whale.

What it does not see yet. Snipers — who bought in the first blocks — and who funded which wallet. Both need the token's transaction history, which is a different and much heavier set of calls. A clean result here means these specific checks passed, not that a token is safe.

Method

What each check actually measures

Every number on the result screen comes from a specific call to a Solana node. Here is what each one reads, and what it cannot tell you.

Mint authority

Read from the mint account itself. If an address is still set here, whoever holds it can create new tokens whenever they want and dilute everyone who already bought. Revoked means the field is empty and no new supply can ever be created.

Freeze authority

Also on the mint account. If it is set, that address can freeze individual token accounts — meaning your wallet can be blocked from selling while others keep trading. This is the check most people forget, and it is the one that traps you after you have bought.

Top 10 holders

The twenty largest token accounts are fetched, each one's owner wallet is resolved, and any account owned by a program rather than a person is marked as a liquidity pool and excluded. What remains is real holder concentration. A pool holding most of the supply is normal; ten wallets holding a third of it is not.

Largest holder

The single biggest non-pool wallet. Useful because a top-ten figure can hide one wallet doing all the damage.

Bundled wallets

Several token accounts in the top twenty can belong to the same owner wallet. When that happens outside a pool, it usually means one person split their position to look like several holders. Clearing counts those accounts and flags each one in the table.

Snipers — not measured

Who bought in the first blocks after launch. This needs the token's full transaction history, back to its oldest signature. That is thousands of paginated calls for any token with real volume, which is not something a browser can do. It would take an indexing service, and until Clearing uses one, this box stays empty rather than showing a number that was guessed.

The score

Starts at 100 and loses points for each armed risk: a live mint authority costs 40, a live freeze authority 30, heavy top-ten concentration 20, an oversized single holder 15, and bundled wallets 20. Every deduction maps to a check you can see. When some checks could not run, the ring shows how many did instead of a score — a token with two passed checks should never display a reassuring 100.

About

A scanner that admits what it does not know

Clearing exists to answer one question before you buy: can somebody still do something to this token that you cannot undo?

✓What it never does

No wallet connection. No signature. No transaction. No account, no email, no API key. The tool cannot touch your money because it never gets near it.

✕What it will not fake

When a data source is unavailable, the box says so. No invented sniper counts, no score out of 100 built on half the checks, no list of "verified" tokens that nobody verified.

Where the data comes from

  • Solana public nodes — the mint account, supply, largest token accounts and their owners. Requested through this site's own relay so your browser is not blocked by cross-origin rules.
  • DexScreener — token name, image, price and the social links shown in the bottom bar, plus the Trending feed.
  • Binance — the BTC, ETH and SOL prices in the bottom bar, over a public websocket.

The current limitation

Free public Solana nodes refuse getTokenLargestAccounts, the call that lists a token's biggest holders. Authority checks still run on every token, but concentration and bundling depend on that call. Connecting a dedicated RPC endpoint switches those checks back on without any other change.

What a clean result means

That the specific mechanisms Clearing can read are not armed. It is not a verdict on the project, the team, or the price. Most tokens lose all their value, including ones that pass every check here. This is not financial advice.
